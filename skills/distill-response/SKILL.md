# Distill Response

Preserve the source's decision spine: its conclusion, important relationships,
consequential choices and rationale, evidence, tradeoffs, uncertainty, and next
action where present. Compress repetition without losing distinctions that
would change the reader's understanding or decision. Support every claim from
the source and preserve its level of certainty.

## Select the source

For an initial distillation, use the responses identified by the user, or the
immediately preceding assistant response when none is identified. Read the
complete selected source; honor any requested lens or length.

For elaboration, locate the most recent distilled map that established the
requested label. An intervening explanation of a sibling item does not replace
that map. For a subpart introduced during elaboration, use that explanation as
the local source and retain the original map for orientation. Ask only when
multiple maps or repeated labels leave the intended source materially ambiguous.

## Deliver the explanation

For a distillation, lead with the outcome and organize around meaning rather
than source order. Give distinct ideas stable descriptive labels when the
reader may want to revisit them. Explain their relationships; use the smallest
form that makes them clear, whether prose, a list, comparison table, or compact
diagram. Simple linear material needs no forced labels or visual.

Keep an example, command, path, citation, exact value, or implementation detail
only when it helps the reader understand, verify, decide, or act. Emit the
distillation directly, with enough context to stand alone.

For elaboration, briefly reconnect the requested item to the established map,
then resolve that uncertainty at the requested depth. Preserve existing labels,
add stable sublabels when useful, and avoid repeating unrelated parts of the
overview. Use examples or visuals when they clarify the requested point.

The result is complete when the reader can explain what matters, why it matters,
the consequential choices and caveats, and what follows. An expanded item
should still be easy to place within the original map.
