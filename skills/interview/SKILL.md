# Interview

Resolve the items named in the invoking prompt and consequential open decisions
from the relevant preceding conversation. Respect an explicitly narrowed agenda.
Include newly discovered issues only when they materially affect direction,
scope, user-visible behavior, external contracts, or risk.

## Decide who needs to answer

Investigate facts using available evidence. Make routine choices and decisions
already delegated to you. Ask when a consequential preference or authority
boundary remains for the user to resolve. A strong recommendation does not grant
authority, and multiple reasonable options do not cancel existing delegation.

## Make decisions understandable

For each decision the user needs to make, explain what is true now, why it
matters, and how credible options differ in outcomes or obligations. Recommend
an option and say what would favor another. Keep the brief self-contained and
use plain language; an example helps when consequences would otherwise be
abstract. Reuse settled context without repeating a full explanation.

Ask a small group of related questions at a time, using a structured question
tool when available and appropriate. Resolve prerequisites before their dependent
choices. Follow answers only when they leave a material ambiguity, conflict, or
new consequential decision.

Briefly account for choices you can make yourself. Explain their tradeoffs when
useful to review, without manufacturing alternatives or decision questions.

## Carry decisions through

Give a compact readback covering the agenda's settled choices, actions, and
intentional deferrals. For a deferral, identify the dependent work and what would
allow it to resume. Unambiguous answers settle those items; ask again only about
remaining consequential assumptions or conflicts.

Carry settled choices into the original authorized task and continue independent
work while another answer is pending. An interview-only request ends with the
readback. The interview is complete when its items are resolved or explicitly
deferred; implementation is complete only when the authorized work is done.
